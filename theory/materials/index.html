    Materials: isotropic and anisotropic media

    Conventions as everywhere in the project: time dependence \(e^{-i\omega t}\), so a lossy medium has \(\operatorname{Im}\varepsilon > 0\); relative parameters \(\varepsilon_r\), \(\mu_r\) (SI otherwise). The decision record is ADR-0015; the stages of the migration are milestone M19 of the roadmap.

    The core material

    materials::Material (include/hpfem/materials/material.hpp) is the frequency-independent description of one medium at one frequency:

    • isotropic: a complex scalar \(\varepsilon_r\) and a complex scalar \(\mu_r\) (Material(eps, mu), Material::isotropic, Material::dielectric(n));
    • anisotropic: a complex symmetric \(3\times3\) permittivity tensor in the laboratory frame and a scalar \(\mu_r\) (Material::anisotropic(eps, mu)).

    The permeability is always scalar: optical crystals have \(\mu_r = 1\), and the full \(\mu^{-1}\) tensors of the assembly are used by the PML only.

    accessor isotropic anisotropic
    is_isotropic() true false
    eps_tensor() \(\varepsilon_r I\) the tensor
    mu_inv_tensor() \(\mu_r^{-1} I\) \(\mu_r^{-1} I\)
    eps_scalar(), refractive_index() \(\varepsilon_r\), \(\sqrt{\varepsilon_r\mu_r}\) throw AnisotropicUnsupported
    mu_scalar() \(\mu_r\) \(\mu_r\)

    Why the scalar accessors throw. Every feature written before M19 reads the scalar permittivity. A tensor material reaching such a feature must not be reduced silently to some scalar (its trace, its \(zz\) entry, …): the result would look plausible and be wrong. The exception (hpfem::AnisotropicUnsupported, a NotImplemented; in Python hpfem.AnisotropicUnsupported, a NotImplementedError) names the accessor, and each feature is migrated to the tensor accessors in its own stage. The Python entry points that run diagnostics (hpfem.grating.validate / solve, diagnostics.validate_scattering) report the error anisotropic_unsupported with the offending tags before the set-up starts.

    Admissible tensors. Material::anisotropic refuses a non-finite or zero tensor and a non-symmetric one, \(\lVert\varepsilon - \varepsilon^T\rVert_{\max} > 10^{-12}\lVert\varepsilon \rVert_{\max}\): antisymmetric parts describe gyrotropic or optically active media, which break reciprocity and are out of scope (ADR-0015 §3). A complex symmetric tensor covers every non-gyrotropic crystal, lossy or not, in any orientation. A tensor proportional to the identity stays a tensor material; it is the isotropic limit with which the tensor paths are checked against the scalar ones.

    MaterialMap::is_isotropic() tells whether a map (background, tags and cell overrides) contains a tensor material.

    Crystal orientation

    A crystal is described by its principal permittivities \(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \varepsilon_3\) (each a dispersive model in Python) and the rotation \(R\) from its principal frame to the laboratory frame (\(x, y\) along the lattice, \(z\) the layer normal of the 3D and the stack solvers):

    \[ \varepsilon_{\text{lab}} = R\,\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \varepsilon_3)\,R^T . \]

    \(R\) is a real rotation, so \(\varepsilon_{\text{lab}}\) is symmetric. The columns of \(R\) are the principal axes in laboratory coordinates.

    Uniaxial crystals (\(\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_2 = \varepsilon_o\), \(\varepsilon_3 = \varepsilon_e\)) are oriented by their optic axis \(\hat c = (\sin\theta\cos\phi,\ \sin\theta\sin\phi,\ \cos\theta)\): \(\theta\) is the polar angle from the laboratory \(z\) axis, \(\phi\) the azimuth from \(x\) towards \(y\), and \(R = R_z(\phi)\,R_y(\theta)\) (materials.axis_rotation). Then \(\varepsilon_{\text{lab}} = \varepsilon_o I + (\varepsilon_e - \varepsilon_o)\,\hat c\hat c^T\). A c-cut substrate has \(\theta = 0\); an a- or m-cut has \(\theta = 90°\), with \(\phi\) the angle of the axis against the \(x\) direction (for a grating, against the direction across the lines).

    Biaxial crystals use intrinsic z-x-z Euler angles, \(R = R_z(\alpha)\,R_x(\beta)\,R_z(\gamma)\) (materials.euler_rotation): first a turn by \(\gamma\) about the principal \(z\) axis, then by \(\beta\) about the new \(x\) axis, then by \(\alpha\) about the laboratory \(z\) axis. With \(\beta = 0\) the rotation is a turn by \(\alpha + \gamma\) about \(z\).

    Volume solvers (2D / 3D)

    Since M19 S2 the volume solvers physics::Scattering, physics::Resonance and physics::BandStructure take tensor materials (by tag, as background, or per cell with MaterialMap::set_cell, e.g. for graded or strained crystals). All three get their coefficients from physics::set_material_tensors (include/hpfem/physics/material_form.hpp); isotropic cells keep the scalar path, a tensor cell enters the Maxwell forms through materials::permittivity_tensor<Dim>:

    • 3D: the full tensor.
    • 2D: the in-plane block \(\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\), \(\alpha, \beta \in \{x, y\}\). The 2D space carries the in-plane field \((E_x, E_y)\) with \(\partial_z = 0\); a tensor with \(\varepsilon_{xz} \ne 0\) or \(\varepsilon_{yz} \ne 0\) makes the in-plane field drive \(E_z\), which that space does not have, and is refused with AnisotropicUnsupported (use the conical solver, M19 S3, or the 3D solver). \(\varepsilon_{zz}\) does not enter.

    Scattered-field source. With an isotropic background \(\varepsilon_b\) the source is the matrix product \(k_0^2(\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_b I)E^{inc}\); the anisotropic layered background is M19 S4.

    PML. The coordinate stretch maps any tensor like a coordinate transformation, $\(\tilde\varepsilon_{ij} = \varepsilon_{ij}\,\frac{s_x s_y s_z}{s_i s_j}\)$ (PmlBox::permittivity(tensor, x), in 2D with \(s_z = 1\) on the in-plane block); \(\tilde\varepsilon\) stays symmetric, so the operator stays complex-symmetric and detect_symmetry keeps the LDLᵀ paths. The PML resolution check uses the largest principal index (materials::max_real_index).

    Stability of the PML in a crystal. The stretch absorbs a wave only if its phase and its group velocity point into the layer together, \(k_d\,v_{g,d} \ge 0\) for the stretched axis \(d\) (Bécache, Fauqueux & Joly 2003). pml::backward_wave<Dim>(eps, axis) checks this on sampled wave directions (2D: slowness curve \(k^TR^T\varepsilon^{-1}Rk = k_0^2\) of the in-plane waves, group velocity \(R^T\varepsilon^{-1}Rk\); 3D: both sheets of the Fresnel surface, group velocity along the Poynting vector \(\hat k|E|^2 - E(\hat k\cdot E)\) of the eigenwave). The result:

    medium in the layer condition
    isotropic holds
    uniaxial, optic axis along the layer normal or perpendicular to it holds
    uniaxial, optic axis tilted against the normal (also an ordinary crystal) fails
    biaxial, principal axes aligned: normal along the largest or smallest axis holds
    biaxial, principal axes aligned: normal along the intermediate axis fails (weakly)

    The last row is the cone of internal conical refraction: near the optic axes, which lie in the plane of the largest and the smallest principal axis, the group velocity of one sheet sweeps a cone that contains \(v_{g,y} < 0\) for \(k_y > 0\) (\(\min k_y v_y \approx -0.01\) for \(\varepsilon = \operatorname{diag}(2, 3, 4.5)\), confirmed by the gradient of \(\omega(k)\)). Scattering logs the warning pml-backward-wave for every crystal and stretched axis that fails, and diagnostics.validate_scattering reports pml_backward_wave. A wave that is not excited is not amplified: an eigenwave along the normal of a tilted crystal is absorbed as usual (tests/convergence/pml_reflection.cpp); the failure matters for grazing and scattered waves.

    Losses. The time-averaged Joule heating of a symmetric tensor is $\(p = \tfrac12\,\omega\varepsilon_0\,E^H\,\operatorname{Im}(\varepsilon)\,E,\)$ because the Hermitian part \((\varepsilon - \varepsilon^H)/2i\) of a symmetric \(\varepsilon\) is the real matrix \(\operatorname{Im}\varepsilon\) (materials::loss_density); it is used by absorbed_power, the per-cell and per-tag absorption, the absorption density and the heat source of physics::Thermal. BandStructure requires a real tensor.

    Estimators. The residual and goal-oriented estimators already took \(\varepsilon E\) from the form, so the divergence term and the jump of \(n\cdot\varepsilon E\) use the tensor without further change.

    The conical solver takes crystals since M19 S3 (full tensors in ConicalForm, the loss of symmetry for \(\varepsilon_{xz}, \varepsilon_{yz} \ne 0\) and the transposed adjoints: maxwell.md); diagnostics.validate_scattering checks conical setups for the scalar \(E_z\) path and the PML condition (the 3D condition, conservative). ConicalSweep and hpfem.grating.solve / validate take crystals in the structured parts; hpfem.grating expects them in the laboratory frame of this page and turns them into the conical frame (x across the lines, y the layer normal, z along the lines) by swapping y and z (ADR-0015 S3 amendment); a MaterialMap with crystals is refused there. Crystal layer stacks are AnisotropicStack (M19 S4) and, in the laboratory frame as well, the stack of hpfem.grating.solve / validate / jones (S5c, with mode-resolved transmitted orders and the modal bottom boundary). The emission paths of hpfem.grating take crystals as well (S7, the emitter in an isotropic host), the axisymmetric solver diagonal tensors in (r, φ, z) (S6: an optic axis along z, or radially / azimuthally anisotropic media; tilted axes are refused). Still isotropic only (they refuse with AnisotropicUnsupported), among others: the dipole host medium, the background and the layered background of the axisymmetric solver, TimeDomain, ThermoOptical, PropagatingMode and the modal ports.

    Python layer

    hpfem.materials.Anisotropic(principal, rotation) holds three dispersive models (numbers and library names are accepted) and the rotation; eps_tensor(omega) returns the laboratory tensor, at(omega) the core tensor Material, rotated(R) the crystal turned by a further laboratory rotation. Uniaxial(ordinary, extraordinary, theta, phi) builds the uniaxial case. The scalar eps_r(omega) and refractive_index(omega) of the Dispersive interface raise AnisotropicUnsupported. DispersiveMap accepts anisotropic models and tensor materials.

    The library entry Al2O3 (sapphire, Malitson & Dodge 1972, ordinary and extraordinary Sellmeier forms, 0.2–5 µm) is a c-cut uniaxial crystal; at 589.3 nm it gives \(n_o = 1.7681\), \(n_e = 1.7600\).

    from hpfem import materials, units
omega = units.angular_frequency(wavelength=633 * units.nm)
a_cut = materials.get("Al2O3").rotated(materials.axis_rotation(theta=np.pi / 2, phi=0.0))
crystal = materials.Uniaxial(2.0, 2.3, theta=0.4, phi=0.2)    # permittivities, radians
eps = crystal.eps_tensor(omega)                                # 3x3, symmetric
material = crystal.at(omega)                                   # tensor hpfem.Material

    Verification

    tests/unit/materials/test_material.cpp: the tensor accessors of both kinds, the refused scalar access, the isotropic limit staying a tensor, the refused gyrotropic, non-finite and zero tensors, MaterialMap::is_isotropic over background, tags and cell overrides. python/tests/test_units_materials.py: the uniaxial tensor \(\varepsilon_o I + (\varepsilon_e - \varepsilon_o)\hat c\hat c^T\) for an axis in the plane, the optic axis as the extraordinary eigenvector for a tilted axis, the Euler rotation against the axis rotation and the principal values of a biaxial tensor, the sapphire indices at 589.3 nm, DispersiveMap with tensors. python/tests/test_diagnostics.py: the error anisotropic_unsupported of grating.validate / solve and of validate_isotropic. The migration itself (private scalar fields, accessors at every former use) is covered by the whole existing test suite.

    Volume solvers (S2): tests/unit/physics/test_anisotropic_volume.cpp — \(\varepsilon_r I\) through the tensor path reproduces the scalar path to round-off in 2D and 3D (solution, residual estimate, absorbed power), \(\varepsilon_{zz}\) does not enter 2D and \(\varepsilon_{xx}\) does, the refusal of plane-to-\(z\) coupling in 2D, the PML stretch of a full tensor, per-cell overrides, the complex symmetry of the operator with an absorbing tilted crystal in the PML (and its loss with a gyrotropic form), and the stability condition of the table above. tests/convergence/pml_reflection.cpp — eigenwaves of a crystal with a tilted axis (2D with \(\varepsilon_{xy} \ne 0\), 3D full tensor, both sheets) through the PML converge exponentially in \(p\) with no reflection floor. tests/convergence/anisotropic_slab.cpp — a uniaxial plate in 3D (axis in the plane at 30°) at normal incidence: the reflected Jones entries \(r_{xx}\), \(r_{yx}\) converge exponentially in \(p\) to the two Fabry–Pérot problems with \(n_e\) and \(n_o\). python/tests/test_anisotropic_volume.py — the bound pipeline and the diagnostics pml_backward_wave and anisotropic_unsupported of validate_scattering.
