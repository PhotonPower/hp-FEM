    Anisotropic layer stacks

    Plane-wave reflection and transmission of planar stacks with crystal layers and a crystal substrate (physics::AnisotropicStack, include/hpfem/physics/anisotropic_stack.hpp; decision record ADR-0015 §5, milestone M19 S4). Conventions as everywhere: time dependence \(e^{-i\omega t}\), a lossy medium has a positive semi-definite \(\operatorname{Im}\varepsilon\); laboratory frame with \(z\) the layer normal (materials.md). The wave comes from an isotropic, lossless incidence medium above the top interface and travels towards \(-z\). The isotropic LayerStack (maxwell.md) stays the fast path of the solvers and the reference of the isotropic case.

    The 4×4 system

    With the in-plane wavevector \(k_0(a, b)\) fixed by the incidence, every field in a homogeneous region is \(F(z)\,e^{ik_0(ax + by)}\). Write \(\tilde H = Z_0H\), so that \(\nabla\times E = ik_0\tilde H\) and \(\nabla\times\tilde H = -ik_0\varepsilon E\). The \(z\) rows are algebraic, $$ \tilde H_z = aE_y - bE_x, \qquad E_z = \frac{-(a\tilde H_y - b\tilde H_x) - \varepsilon_{zx}E_x - \varepsilon_{zy}E_y}{\varepsilon_{zz}}, $$ (this needs \(\varepsilon_{zz} \ne 0\); an epsilon-near-zero \(\varepsilon_{zz}\) is refused), and the tangential field \(\psi = (E_x, E_y, \tilde H_x, \tilde H_y)\) obeys the Berreman system (Berreman 1972, Yeh 1979) $$ \psi' = ik_0K\psi, \qquad \begin{aligned} E_x' &= ik_0(\tilde H_y + aE_z), & E_y' &= ik_0(bE_z - \tilde H_x),\ \tilde H_x' &= ik_0\big(a\tilde H_z - (\varepsilon E)_y\big), & \tilde H_y' &= ik_0\big(b\tilde H_z + (\varepsilon E)_x\big). \end{aligned} $$ AnisotropicStack::berreman_matrix builds \(K\) column by column from this linear map. Its eigenvalues are the normalised vertical wavenumbers \(q = k_z/k_0\); for an isotropic medium they are \(\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon - a^2 - b^2}\), each twice. The power flux along \(z\) is \(2Z_0S_z = \operatorname{Re}(E_x\bar{\tilde H}_y - E_y\bar{\tilde H}_x)\).

    Subspaces instead of eigenvectors

    The eigenvectors of \(K\) are not a reliable basis: every isotropic region is doubly degenerate, an optic axis near \(z\) or near-normal incidence makes individual eigenvectors ill-conditioned (the eigenvector matrix of a degenerate region can be numerically singular), and absorbing biaxial media have Voigt (exceptional) points where \(K\) is defective. The stack therefore uses, per region, two ordered complex Schur forms \(K = UTU^H\) (LAPACK ztrexc-style Givens swaps of adjacent diagonal entries): one with the two upward eigenvalues first, one with the two downward ones first. The leading two columns of \(U\) are an orthonormal basis \(W_u\) (resp. \(W_d\)) of the upward (downward) invariant subspace, and the leading \(2\times2\) block \(T_u\) (\(T_d\)) is \(K\) on that subspace, upper triangular. Both stay well defined under degeneracy and at a defective point.

    Up and down. An eigenvalue is upward if \(\operatorname{Im}q > 0\) (decaying upwards: evanescent or absorbing); for \(\operatorname{Im}q = 0\) (propagating in a lossless medium) by the sign of \(S_z\) of its eigenvector — the only use of eigenvectors, and a sign does not suffer from their conditioning. A region without two modes of each kind (grazing, \(k_z = 0\)) is refused.

    Propagation. In region \(j\) of thickness \(d\) the upward amplitudes are referred to its bottom, the downward ones to its top, so the propagators $$ P_u = \exp(ik_0dT_u), \qquad P_d = \exp(-ik_0dT_d) $$ only decay (or keep their modulus). They are the closed-form exponentials of upper triangular \(2\times2\) matrices, \(\exp\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1 & \beta\\ 0 & \lambda_2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}e^{\lambda_1} & \beta\,\frac{e^{\lambda_1} - e^{\lambda_2}}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_2}\\ 0 & e^{\lambda_2}\end{pmatrix}\), with the divided difference by its Taylor series for \(|\lambda_1 - \lambda_2| < 10^{-3}\) — exact at a defective block.

    S-matrix recursion. At interface \(i\) (region \(i\) above, \(i+1\) below) the upward amplitudes are \(\alpha_i = R_i\delta_i\) (all at the interface). Below, the upward field of region \(i+1\) at the interface is \(\Gamma\delta_{i+1}\) with \(\Gamma = P_uR_{i+1}P_d\) (\(\Gamma = 0\) in the substrate). Continuity of \(\psi\), $$ \begin{pmatrix} W_u^{(i)} & -\big(W_u^{(i+1)}\Gamma + W_d^{(i+1)}\big) \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}\alpha_i\ \delta_{i+1}\end{pmatrix} = -W_d^{(i)}\delta_i , $$ gives \(R_i\) and the transmission \(\delta_{i+1} = \tau_i\delta_i\) by one \(4\times4\) solve per interface, from the substrate upwards. No transfer matrix crosses a layer: thick and absorbing layers neither overflow nor cancel (a 100-wavelength absorbing crystal reflects like the half-space).

    Output

    For incident s (\(E \parallel \hat s = \hat z\times\hat k_\parallel\)) and p (\(E \parallel \hat s\times\hat k\), so that \(H \parallel +\hat s\) as in LayerStack) of unit \(|E|\): the downward amplitudes \(\delta_0 = W_d^{(0)H}\psi_{inc}\), the reflected field \(W_u^{(0)}R_0\delta_0\) decomposed on the reflected s and p waves gives the Jones matrix $$ \begin{pmatrix} r_{ss} & r_{sp}\ r_{ps} & r_{pp}\end{pmatrix} $$ (columns incident, rows reflected; E amplitudes at the top interface), the transmitted field at the top of the substrate is decomposed on the two downward substrate modes (s and p of an isotropic substrate; the eigenvectors of \(T_d\) for a crystal, or the orthonormal Schur basis where the modes coincide), each with unit \(|E|\). Reflectance and transmittance follow from \(S_z\) of the incident, reflected and transmitted fields, \(A = 1 - R - T\).

    For an isotropic stack \(r_{ss}\), \(r_{pp}\) equal LayerStack's s and p reflection coefficients (the p one is the ratio of the H amplitudes, which with this p vector equals the ratio of the E amplitudes in the incidence medium) and \(r_{sp} = r_{ps} = 0\). At normal incidence and azimuth 0, \(\hat s = \hat y\), the incident p is \(-\hat x\) and the reflected p is \(+\hat x\), so \(r_{xx} = -r_{pp}\), \(r_{yx} = -r_{sp}\), \(r_{yy} = r_{ss}\), \(r_{xy} = r_{ps}\).

    Substrate modes

    The transmitted field is decomposed on two downward modes of the substrate, each with unit \(|E|\) (StackResponse::substrate_modes, substrate_mode_kinds):

    • isotropic: s and p;
    • uniaxial (two equal principal values of \(\operatorname{Re}\varepsilon\), optic axis \(\hat c\) the third eigenvector): the ordinary mode is the one with \(D = \varepsilon E \perp \hat c\) (its \(k_z = -k_0\sqrt{\varepsilon_o - a^2 - b^2}\)), the extraordinary one the other eigenmode of the downward block;
    • biaxial: the two eigenvectors of the downward block \(T_d\);
    • coinciding modes (k along an optic axis): the basis of the downward subspace that diagonalises the Hermitian cross power flux $P_{ij} = \tfrac12(\bar E_{x,i}\tilde H_{y,j} + \bar{\tilde H}{y,i}E{x,j} - \bar E_{y,i}\tilde H_{x,j}
    • \bar{\tilde H}{x,i}E{y,j})$ (power-orthogonal modes).

    channel_transmittance is the power per mode, \(|t_m|^2\) times the mode's flux over the incident flux; it adds up to \(T\) when the modes are power-orthogonal (always for a lossless substrate).

    The stack as layered background

    AnisotropicStack::plane_wave(k0, angle, pol, amplitude, azimuth) returns the analytic total field in every region, $$ \psi(z) = e^{ik_0(ax + by)}\Big(W_u\,e^{ik_0T_u(z - z_b)}\alpha + W_d\,e^{ik_0T_d(z - z_t)}\delta\Big), $$ with the upward amplitudes at the bottom and the downward ones at the top of each region (so all exponentials decay), the full E from \(\psi\) and \(\nabla\times E = ik_0\tilde H\), the branch of a given region (value_in_region, for points on an interface) and the incident wave alone. The incident wave has the phase 1 at the origin of the top interface, as in LayerStack.

    3D solver. layer_stack() gives the geometry as a LayerStack<3> (which now holds crystal layers for lookup; its own scalar plane_wave refuses them). With it as ScatteringSetup<3>::background and the stack field as incident, the scattered-field source is the tensor contrast \(k_0^2(\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_b)E^{inc}\), zero wherever a cell equals its layer.

    Conical solver. anisotropic_conical_wave(stack, k0, angle, azimuth, pol) turns a stack given in the laboratory frame into the solver frame of the conical solver (x across the lines, y the normal, z along them): the background LayerStack<2> carries \(P\varepsilon P^T\) with P swapping y and z, the solver point \((x, y)\) is the laboratory point \((x, 0, y)\), the field is \(PE\) in scaled components and its physical curl \(\det(P)\,P\,\nabla\times E = -P\,\nabla\times E\) (the curl is a pseudovector and P a reflection). The angles and the s / p vectors coincide with layered_conical_wave.

    Mode-resolved diffraction orders

    half_space_modes(eps, a, b, upward) gives the two upward or downward modes of a homogeneous half-space at the normalised in-plane wavevector \((a, b)\), labelled as the substrate modes above, with unit \(|E|\), their \(k_z/k_0\), the full \(E\) and the flux \(2Z_0S_z\) along their direction. The substrate modes of response are the downward ones.

    conical_mode_orders (ADR-0015 §7) decomposes the orders of a conical field on an OrderLine in a crystal on these modes. With the unit tangent \(\hat t\) and the normal \(\hat n\) away from the structure, the local frame \((\hat t, \hat n\times\hat t, \hat n)\) is right-handed in the solver coordinates; with R the matrix of its rows the order \(m\) sees the medium \(R\varepsilon R^T\) at \(a = k_{t,m}/k_0\), \(b = \beta\,(\hat n\times\hat t)_z/k_0\), and only outgoing waves (along \(\hat n\)) on the line. The tangential part of \(R A_m\) then fixes both amplitudes \(c_{1,2}\) by a 2×2 solve, and

    \[ \eta_{m,j} = \frac{|c_j|^2 S_j}{|E_0|^2 k_n^{inc}/k_0}, \qquad \eta_m = \frac{S(c_1\psi_1 + c_2\psi_2)}{|E_0|^2 k_n^{inc}/k_0} \]

    with the fluxes \(S\) in units of \(2Z_0\). In a lossless medium with distinct modes the channels are power-orthogonal and \(\eta_m = \eta_{m,1} + \eta_{m,2}\); in an isotropic one the channels are s and p and \(\eta_m\) equals conical_diffraction_orders. In a lossy medium the efficiencies are the powers through the line. A channel propagates when \(|\operatorname{Im}k_n| < |\operatorname{Re}k_n|\).

    Jones convention and reciprocity. The reflection Jones matrix of the stack uses \(\hat s = \hat z\times\hat k_\parallel\) for both waves and \(\hat p = \hat s\times\hat k/n\) in the laboratory frame. hpfem.grating.jones uses conical_polarisation in the solver frame, \(\hat s = \hat k\times\hat n\), \(\hat p = \hat s\times\hat k\); mapped back by the reflection P (y ↔ z), cross products change sign, so its ŝ is the stack's and its p̂ the negative of the stack's for both waves: \(J = D\,r\,D\) with \(D = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)\), i.e. equal diagonals and opposite off-diagonal elements (checked to \(10^{-8}\) on a flat crystal stack). Reversing the specular path at the azimuth φ is the incidence at φ + π, and for every symmetric tensor

    \[ r_{ss}(\phi + \pi) = r_{ss}(\phi),\quad r_{pp}(\phi + \pi) = r_{pp}(\phi),\quad r_{ps}(\phi) = -r_{sp}(\phi + \pi). \]

    A plate with its axis in the layer plane at the azimuth α against the plane of incidence is mirrored by α → −α: \(r_{ss}, r_{pp}\) are even, \(r_{sp}, r_{ps}\) odd in α (and zero for the axis in or perpendicular to the plane of incidence).

    Modal transparent boundary

    A coordinate-stretching PML amplifies a backward wave, whose phase travels into the layer while its energy leaves it (\(k_nv_n < 0\), pml-backward-wave). In a crystal with a tilted axis an outgoing diffraction order can be such a wave. ConicalScatteringSetup::modal_boundaries truncates the substrate (or the cover) of a periodic conical problem instead with the exact radiation condition of the half-space: the boundary term of the weak form, \(\oint (n\times\nabla\times E)\cdot F\,ds = ik_0\oint (n\times\tilde H)\cdot F\,ds\) (\(\nabla\times E = ik_0\tilde H\), the test function in physical components \(F = (V_x, V_y, -iw)\,e^{-i\beta z}\)), is evaluated order by order. In the local frame \((\hat t, \hat n\times\hat t, \hat n)\) of the line (n outward) the unknown is outgoing, so for the order \(k_m = k_{t0} + 2\pi m/a\) its tangential magnetic field is \(\tilde H_t = Y_mE_t\) with the admittance \(Y_m = W_HW_E^{-1}\) of the two outgoing modes of half_space_modes (labelling-independent, also for coinciding modes). With \(J = \left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\) for \(n\times\),

    \[ B_{ij} = ik_0\sum_{m=-M}^{M}\hat F_{i,m}^T\,J\,Y_m\,\hat E_{j,m},\qquad \hat E_{j,m} = \frac1a\int_\Gamma E_t(\phi_j)e^{-ik_ms}ds,\quad \hat F_{i,m} = \int_\Gamma F_t(\phi_i)e^{ik_ms}ds \]

    (conical_modal_boundary_matrix, dense on the DoFs of the line; the tangential traces are the tangential Nédélec component and \(\pm E_z = \pm iv\)). The Bloch constraints combine the two ends of the line as for the volume terms. Orders beyond M see a natural boundary; the default \(M = \lceil n_fp/2\rceil + 2\) covers the resolution of the trace of n_f facets of order p. Like the volume operator, B satisfies \(B(\beta, k_t)^T = S\,B(-\beta, -k_t)\,S\) (reciprocity of a symmetric tensor). The line must be straight, along the period, in one homogeneous non-magnetic medium outside the PML; the estimators, the resonance and the sweep solvers do not take the term.

    Crystal stacks in hpfem.grating

    grating.solve / validate / jones take the stack as an AnisotropicStack in the laboratory frame (as the crystals of the structured parts); it is turned into the solver frame with anisotropic_conical_wave. A LayerStack2D with crystals is refused (its frame would be ambiguous). In a crystal substrate the transmitted orders are measured with conical_mode_orders and carry channels; bottom="modal" truncates the substrate by the modal transparent boundary, and bottom="pml" is refused when a transmitted order is a backward wave. grating.jones solves for s and p and returns per propagating order the power-normalised Jones matrix (amplitudes referred to the top / bottom interface of the stack, rows s / p or the mode channels) and its Mueller matrix \(M = A(J\otimes\bar J)A^{-1}\) (Stokes vectors (I, Q, U, V), \(Q = |E_s|^2 - |E_p|^2\); \(M_{00}\) is the efficiency for unpolarised light). The reciprocity relation above holds for the specular order of a grating too (the reversed path is the incidence at φ + π).

    Form birefringence. A lamellar grating of period P ≪ λ (fill f, lines along y) reflects like the uniaxial plate of effective-medium theory, \(\varepsilon_y = \varepsilon_z = f\varepsilon_1 + (1 - f)\varepsilon_2\) along the lines and the normal, \(\varepsilon_x = (f/\varepsilon_1 + (1 - f)/\varepsilon_2)^{-1}\) across them (component by component for crystal lamellae with the axes of the lamellae; L. Li 2003). The plate's reflection refers to its top, the grating's to the bottom of the lines: \(J = e^{-2ik_0\cos\theta\,h}DrD\). The difference falls like P/λ, not like the (P/λ)² of the bulk correction: the evanescent orders form boundary layers of thickness ~P at the top and the bottom of the grating, which the plate lacks. The Richardson extrapolation \(2J(P/2) - J(P)\) removes that term and falls like (P/λ)².

    A dipole above the stack

    AnisotropicStack::reflection_at(k0, a, b) gives the Jones reflection matrix at any in-plane wavevector \(k_0(a, b)\), also beyond the light cone of the cover, where the incident and the reflected wave are evanescent (\(q = \sqrt{n^2 - a^2 - b^2}\), Im q ≥ 0, ŝ and p̂ complex with \(\hat s\cdot\hat s = \hat p\cdot\hat p = 1\)). A current moment m at the height h above the top interface delivers (exp(−iωt), Weyl expansion of the Green's dyadic, \(I - \hat k\hat k^T = \hat s\hat s^T + \hat p\hat p^T\))

    \[ \frac{P}{P_{bulk}} = 1 + \frac{3}{4\pi n|m|^2}\,\mathrm{Re}\iint \frac{da\,db}{q}\, e^{2ik_0qh}\; m^H\,[\hat s, \hat p_u]\; r\; [\hat s, \hat p_d]^T\, m, \qquad P_{bulk} = \frac{Z_0k_0^2n|m|^2}{12\pi} \]

    (hpfem.planar.dipole_above_stack). For an isotropic stack and m ∥ ẑ it reduces to the Chance–Prock–Silbey integral \(1 + \tfrac32\mathrm{Re}\int s^3/s_z\,r_p\,e^{2ik_1hs_z}ds\) (the stack's \(r_{pp}\) has the convention \(r_p = +1\) for a perfect conductor). The Gaussian dipole of ADR-0013 multiplies every spectral component by \(e^{-\sigma^2k\cdot k} = e^{-(nk_0\sigma)^2}\), the factor of \(P_{bulk}\), so the Purcell factor of the point dipole is that of the FEM emitter. The radial integral runs on the real axis (\(s = n\sin t\), \(s = n\cosh u\)), the azimuth by the trapezoidal rule. Guided modes of lossless stacks are poles on the real axis (S7e-2a, depth > 0): bound modes are evanescent in both half-spaces, so beyond the largest principal index \(s_b\) of the cover and the substrate the radial path leaves the axis, \(s = s_b + (S - s_b)\tau - i\,\text{depth}\,\sin\pi\tau\) up to S beyond all indices (a small loss moves the poles above the axis, exp(−iωt), so the path passes them on the causal side), with the stack at complex in-plane wavevectors (reflection_at; ŝ normalised by the non-conjugating norm, the two modes with the largest Im k_z upward, unambiguous where both half-spaces are evanescent). Closer to the axis a tilted crystal may have upward modes with Im k_z < 0 (its in-plane group velocity can oppose the wavevector), which is why the path stays real up to \(s_b\).

    One sample of the array scan. The cell problem of grating.emit (a Bloch array of Gaussian dipoles with \(e^{ik_xP}\) and \(e^{i\beta z}\)) samples the same spectrum on the orders \(k_m = k_x + 2\pi m/P\) at \(k_y^{lab} = \beta\): with \(\iint d^2k = \sum_m\int_{BZ}dk_x\int d\beta\) and the array-scan weight \(P/(4\pi^2)\),

    \[ P_{cell}(k_x, \beta) = \frac{\omega\mu_0}{4P}\sum_m \mathrm{Re}\Big[\frac{1}{k_z}\Big( \tfrac12\textstyle\sum_\pm|m_\perp^\pm|^2\,[k_z\ \text{real}] + e^{2ik_zh}\,m^HM_mm\Big)\Big] e^{-(nk_0\sigma)^2} \]

    (hpfem.planar.array_sample_above_stack; the homogeneous term is the closed form of the M17 S1 test). The Gaussian must stay 6σ clear of the stack interfaces (emit warns otherwise): a source reaching into the next medium is no longer the point dipole of the reference (the first comparison missed by 2.5 % for σ = 0.05 µm at 0.12 µm until the dipole was raised to 0.32 µm).

    The array scan above crystals (S7c). The (kx, β) integrand of grating.dipole_emission is even in β and in kx only for mirror-symmetric media. With a crystal in the cell or the stack, β runs over (−β_max, β_max) with half the weight, kx over the full zone, and every entry of the power matrix counts. The light line of a crystal half-space (k_y = 0 in the solver frame) is the zero set of the Fresnel quartic \(F(k_x) = \det(k_0^2\varepsilon + kk^T - |k|^2I)\), \(k = (k_x, 0, \beta)\): its real roots are the crossings of the kx contour, on the side of the group velocity \(v_x = -F_{k_x}/F_{k_0}\) (a small loss moves the root into the upper half plane when \(v_x > 0\)), its complex roots the branch points of the evanescent sheets; β is split at the turning points of the two sheets, the largest \(k\sin t\) of the generalised eigenvalues \(\det(k_0^2\varepsilon - k^2(I - uu^T)) = 0\), \(u = (\cos t, 0, \sin t)\). The Wood-anomaly pinch breaks are kept for the isotropic media only. symmetric is not available with crystals. The guided-mode poles (S7e-2b) are detected per β node by the AAA fit on the kx contour and bumped to the causal side as for isotropic cells, which needs no kx mirror; the β pre-pass that splits the β rule at the pinch points (pole pairs meeting at kx = 0 or π/P) does assume it and is skipped, so a crystal structure's β rule is not split there. This path is not validated yet: benchmarks/m19_crystal_guided_scan.py (a tilted crystal slab against dipole_above_stack(depth=...), a long run) is pending, and dipole_emission warns.

    Emission by reciprocity (S7d). dP/dΩ into the direction r̂ is \(n k_0^2Z_0|p\cdot\langle E_{pw}\rangle|^2/(32\pi^2)\) with the plane wave incident from r̂ (ADR-0013 §6); reciprocity needs only a symmetric ε, so it holds for crystals. A direction into the substrate is computed on the cell mirrored at y = 0: the mirror maps a solution for ε onto one for \(M\varepsilon M\), \(M = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, 1)\) (solver frame), so every cell's tensor is mirrored and the stack becomes the AnisotropicStack of the reversed layers with \(M_z\varepsilon M_z\) (laboratory frame), the former substrate as its incidence medium. A crystal substrate becomes a crystal incidence medium of the mirrored stack (S7e below). The Gaussian average at the dipole carries \(e^{-(n_{host}k_0\sigma)^2}\) for every direction (the plane wave at the dipole lives in the host), the prefactor n is that of the direction's medium.

    Radiation into a crystal substrate (S7e). The stack takes a lossless crystal as its incidence medium; AnisotropicStack::mode_wave(k0, a, b, mode) gives the analytic field of one of its downward modes (half_space_modes: ordinary / extraordinary), and anisotropic_conical_mode_wave the same in the conical solver frame. A direction into a crystal is not described by an index, so the power is integrated over the in-plane wavevector of the substrate modes: with the field ⟨E_j⟩ at the dipole of mode j incident from the substrate with unit |E| and its z flux \(F_j\) (\(2Z_0S_z\)),

    \[ \frac{dP}{d^2k_\parallel} = \frac{Z_0\,|p\cdot\langle E_j\rangle|^2}{32\pi^2F_j}, \]

    which for an isotropic medium is \(dP/d\Omega = nk_0^2Z_0|p\cdot E|^2/(32\pi^2)\) with \(d\Omega = d^2k_\parallel/(kk_z)\) and \(F = k_z/k_0\). Per azimuth ψ the wavenumber runs to the cutoff \(\kappa_{max,j}(\psi)\) of the mode (bisection on half_space_modes) as \(\kappa = \kappa_{max}\sin\tau\) (the \(1/F_j\) singularity cancels), split at the light line of the cover (the near-field coupling peaks just beyond it) and at the cutoff of the other mode (its reflected wave turns evanescent), each panel with the cosine-mapped Gauss rule: a plain Gauss rule converged only like \(n^{-3}\) on the square-root points (0.5 % at 8 points per panel). The incident mode is solved on the mirrored cell (grating._crystal_substrate_radiation); emission_cone(side="down") (the whole half-space) and the channels of dipole_emission use it. Uniaxial substrates only (the o / e labelling); biaxial ones are refused.

    Verification

    tests/unit/physics/test_anisotropic_stack.cpp:

    • isotropic stacks (absorbing layer, a metal film, lossy substrate, three angles at an azimuth) reproduce LayerStack to \(10^{-12}\), with zero cross-polarisation;
    • thin crystal layers (an absorbing tilted biaxial layer, an isotropic one, a tilted uniaxial one) on a tilted uniaxial substrate agree with an independent transfer-matrix reference (\(\exp(-ik_0Kd)\) by Taylor series with scaling and squaring, no eigenvectors in the layers) to \(10^{-10}\), cross-polarisation included;
    • a plate with the axis in the plane at normal incidence gives the two Fabry–Pérot problems with \(n_e\) and \(n_o\) in closed form, \(r_{xx} = \cos^2\phi\,r_e + \sin^2\phi\,r_o\), \(r_{yx} = \sin\phi\cos\phi\,(r_e - r_o)\);
    • an optic axis exactly along \(z\) at normal incidence (both pairs degenerate) equals the isotropic plate with \(n_o\), and \(10^{-6}\) rad off it changes the result by less than \(10^{-9}\); the same for a crystal substrate with the axis along \(z\);
    • lossless crystals conserve energy, \(R + T = 1\) to \(10^{-12}\); a 100-wavelength absorbing crystal reflects as the half-space;
    • at a Voigt point of an absorbing biaxial medium (found by Newton on the discriminant \((q_1 - q_2)^2\) of the downward eigenvalues), the layer agrees with the eigenvector-free reference to \(10^{-9}\), and the half-space reflection is continuous across the point;
    • the analytic field solves \(\nabla\times\nabla\times E = k_0^2\varepsilon E\) in every region (finite differences), its tangential components are continuous at the interfaces, and for an isotropic stack it equals LayerStack's field and curl;
    • the ordinary mode of a tilted uniaxial substrate has the closed-form \(k_z\); the power channels add up to \(T\), also for coinciding modes;
    • tests/convergence/anisotropic_stack_background.cpp: a tilted crystal plate meshed on the analytic half-space of a c-cut substrate converges to the analytic field of the full stack, in the 3D solver (Bloch cell, \(2\cdot10^{-2} \to 9\cdot10^{-5}\) for p = 2…4) and in the conical solver (\(5\cdot10^{-2} \to 1\cdot10^{-5}\) for p = 2…5); a structure equal to the stack has no scattered field (exactly zero), in both solvers;
    • anisotropic_conical_wave of an isotropic stack equals layered_conical_wave (field, curl, β, R), and its curl matches the field by finite differences for a crystal stack;
