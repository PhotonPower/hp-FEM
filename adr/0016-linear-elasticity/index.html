    0016 — Linear elasticity with eigenstrain on a vector H1 space

    Status: accepted (2026-11-19); §4a amendment (2026-11-19, M20 S2) Date: 2026-11-19

    Context

    Issue #213 (proposal from Voxelspaetzle, the two-photon printing process model, its decision D4 of 2026-11-07) asks for the shrinkage of printed polymer parts during development and post-curing: the shape error and focal shift of printed micro-optics, observed for example as a changed dispersion of a printed spectrometer (Toulouse et al., Light Adv. Manuf. 2 (2021) 2). The model is small-strain linear elasticity with an eigenstrain:

    −∇·σ = 0,   σ = C : (ε(u) − ε*),   ε(u) = ½(∇u + ∇uᵀ),   ε* = −s(α) I
    • Shrinkage. s is the isotropic shrinkage; it follows from the degree of polymerisation α, which Voxelspaetzle delivers as a volume field.
    • Material. C is isotropic, given by (E, ν) per element.
    • Boundary conditions. The part is clamped on the substrate face and traction-free elsewhere.
    • Wanted output. The displacement at the nodes and at surface points.
    • Acceptance. Three references: a free sphere with homogeneous shrinkage (exact u = −s r), a bonded thin plate (analytic), and the patch test.

    Facts from the code (main at 115befe):

    • Scalar H1 space. fespace::DofMap<Dim> and H1Basis<Dim> give the hierarchical H1 space with variable order per entity and hanging-node constraints (assembly::hanging_constraints).
    • The vertex functions are the P1 hat functions, so a vertex coefficient is the value at the vertex.
    • assembly::block_constraints combines the constraints of two spaces on a block vector; the axisymmetric solver uses it for its (e | v) block.
    • Model problem. physics::Thermal<Dim> (M9) solves an H1 problem with a per-tag coefficient, Dirichlet data on tagged facets and a load from another field on the same mesh. It is the pattern to follow.
    • Linear algebra is complex only. SparseMatrix is complex, and so are the LinearSolver backends: SparseLU, MUMPS (zmumps) and cuDSS. Symmetry::kComplexSymmetric and kDetect select LDLᵀ in MUMPS and cuDSS. There is no real-valued sparse path.
    • Evaluation. assembly::evaluate_h1 evaluates a scalar H1 field at a reference or physical point (point location), and assembly::StaticCondensation eliminates the cell-interior DoFs.
    • Shape derivatives. ADR-0011 describes a geometry change as a mesh velocity V on the geometry nodes: shape_sensitivity(gradient, V) = Σ ∂Q/∂x · V. A displacement field u on the same mesh is exactly such a V.
    • Data ports. hpfem.dataports.RegularGrid is the regular grid of the data ports in µm in the sensor frame.

    Decision

    1. Formulation

    The unknown is the displacement u ∈ [H¹(Ω)]^Dim, Dim = 3, or 2 in plane strain. The weak form:

    find u with u = g on Γ_D such that for all v with v = 0 on Γ_D:
    ∫ C ε(u) : ε(v) dx = ∫ C ε* : ε(v) dx + ∫ f · v dx + ∫_{Γ_N} t · v ds
    • Eigenstrain. ε* is a general symmetric tensor field. Isotropic shrinkage (−s I) and thermal expansion (α_th ΔT I) are special cases.
    • Stiffness. C is given per cell as the 6×6 Voigt matrix (3×3 in 2D). Isotropic materials enter through (E, ν), with λ = Eν/((1+ν)(1−2ν)) and μ = E/(2(1+ν)). The interface admits anisotropic C later without a change.
    • Units. SI: u in m, E and σ in Pa.
    • Theory. The weak form and the conventions go to docs/theory/elasticity.md.

    Body forces f and tractions t are part of the form from the start. They cost almost nothing, and the manufactured-solution test needs f.

    2. Space and numbering

    The vector space is Dim copies of the existing scalar DofMap<Dim>, with the same orders on every entity.

    • Numbering. The block vector is component-major: [u_x | u_y | u_z].
    • Hanging nodes. The constraints are the scalar ones, repeated per component through block_constraints.
    • Condensation. Static condensation works per cell on the interior DoFs of all components.
    • p-refinement. It changes all components of a cell together.

    No new basis, no new DoF map, and no new constraint code are needed.

    3. Number type

    The system is real symmetric positive definite. We assemble it in Complex, with a zero imaginary part, and solve it with the existing backends under Symmetry::kComplexSymmetric, which selects LDLᵀ in MUMPS and cuDSS.

    • Why. The principle "everything is complex" (CLAUDE.md §2) holds. Elasticity uses the whole solver stack as it is: MUMPS, cuDSS, the kept factorisation and the optimisation layer.
    • Later uses. A complex modulus (viscoelastic damping) or time-harmonic elastodynamics (acousto- and opto-mechanics) need no change of type.
    • Cost. Memory doubles and the factorisation takes about 4× the flops of a real LDLᵀ.
    • Real path. A real-valued backend is an optimisation behind LinearSolver (an amendment to ADR-0007). It is added only when a measured problem needs it.

    4. Boundary conditions

    • Dirichlet per component on tagged facets. A setup entry gives (facet tag, components, value):
    • all components zero clamps the part on the substrate face;
    • one component zero on an axis-aligned plane is a symmetry plane, used for the 1/8 sphere and the laterally constrained layer.

    The elimination is the one of Thermal, done per component. - Neumann. Tractions on tagged facets. Untagged boundaries are traction-free, which is the natural condition. - Free bodies. The rigid-body modes are removed by remove_rigid_modes: 6 Lagrange multipliers in 3D and 3 in 2D, bordering the system, which fix the mean translation and the mean rotation to zero. The bordered system is symmetric indefinite, and the direct solvers handle it. We choose this over pinning single DoFs because it does not depend on the mesh and leaves the stresses untouched.

    4a. Amendment (M20 S2): rigid-body modes by pins and a projection

    §4 asked for Lagrange multipliers. They do not combine cleanly with the static condensation: the mean of u over the cells involves the bubble DoFs, which the condensation eliminates. The implementation reaches the same result in two steps.

    1. Pins. Before the solve, six DoFs in 3D (three in 2D) at three far-apart vertices are set to zero (the 3-2-1 rule). This removes the singular directions.
    2. Projection. After the solve, the L2 projection onto the rigid-body modes is subtracted.
    3. The modes are the translations and the rotations about the centroid of the mesh, interpolated in the space. They are exact on quadratic cells for p ≥ 2.
    4. The solution then has zero mean translation and rotation, so the result does not depend on the mesh, as with the multipliers.

    The loads must be in equilibrium. solve checks that the load is orthogonal to the modes and refuses unbalanced loads. Eigenstrains are always in equilibrium. For a balanced load the pins carry no reaction, so there are no stress concentrations. The pins are not on DoFs constrained by hanging nodes.

    5. Eigenstrain and data input

    The eigenstrain enters as a callable evaluated at the quadrature points: ε*(x) as a 3×3 symmetric matrix, or s(x) for isotropic shrinkage. There are three sources:

    • Python callables. Functions and NumPy-vectorised data.
    • Data port (hpfem.dataports). A volume field on a RegularGrid, for example Voxelspaetzle's α or s on voxels, interpolated trilinearly. Points outside the grid raise an error.
    • Fields on the same mesh. A temperature from Thermal gives the thermal strain α_th (T − T₀) I. This is the thermo-mechanical coupling, at no extra cost.

    The map from α to s, the shrinkage law of the resin, belongs to Voxelspaetzle and is not part of hp-FEM.

    6. Output

    • Displacement. The coefficients, and the vertex values, which are the vertex coefficients of the hierarchical basis.
    • Point values. The displacement at arbitrary points through point location, for example on the surface for the shape error.
    • Strain and stress. ε(u) and σ at points, and the strain energy ½∫ε : C : ε.
    • Deformed mesh. The mesh with its geometry nodes moved by u, for an optical solve on the shrunk part.
    • Mesh velocity. u passed to shape_sensitivity as the ADR-0011 velocity V. This gives the first-order change of an optical quantity caused by the shrinkage without remeshing, for example the focal shift or the phase of a lens.

    7. Locking

    The formulation is purely in displacements. The printed polymers have ν ≈ 0.35–0.45, where the hierarchical space with p ≥ 2 does not lock in practice. The setup warns for ν > 0.45 and refuses ν ≥ 0.5. A mixed u–p formulation is an alternative (see below) and is not part of this decision.

    8. Verification

    These are acceptance criteria. Each is a unit or convergence test as in CLAUDE.md §8.

    1. Patch test. Every linear displacement field, with body force 0 and matching tractions, is reproduced to round-off for p ≥ 1, also on meshes with hanging nodes.
    2. Manufactured solution. A smooth u with the corresponding f and t. The convergence test requires rate p + 1 in L² and p in the energy norm, for p = 1 … 4 on cubes and triangles.
    3. Free sphere with homogeneous shrinkage. The exact field is linear, u = −s r with zero stress. It is reproduced with remove_rigid_modes, and also on the 1/8 sphere with symmetry planes. On curved second-order meshes the geometry error remains, so the test checks against it.
    4. Laterally constrained layer on a rigid substrate. The sides are symmetry planes and the base is clamped. The exact 1D solution is u_z = −s z (1 + ν)/(1 − ν), with in-plane stress σ_xx = σ_yy = s E/(1 − ν).
    5. Bonded finite plate. The film on a substrate (bimetal / Stoney curvature), compared with Timoshenko's analytic plate solution away from the edges. The tolerance is set by the plate-theory error, and the test is documented as a validation in docs/validation.md.

    9. Stages

    A new milestone M20 — Linear elasticity (ADR-0016) in docs/roadmap.md:

    Stage Content
    S1 Vector H1 assembly and physics::Elasticity<Dim> (3D and 2D plane strain): Dirichlet per component, tractions, body force. Tests 1 and 2.
    S2 Eigenstrain fields, the data-port input and remove_rigid_modes. Tests 3 and 4.
    S3 Output: points, stress, energy, the deformed mesh. Python API (hpfem.Elasticity3D / 2D). The bonded plate (test 5). An example: a printed micro-lens or cube shrinking.
    S4 Coupling to optics: an optical solve on the deformed mesh, and u as the shape velocity (focal shift to first order), checked against the optical solve on the deformed mesh.
    S5 (optional) Axisymmetric elasticity (u_r, u_z) for printed lenses, which are often bodies of revolution. A residual estimator and hp-adaptivity for elasticity. A real-valued backend if measured necessary.

    Consequences

    • Little new code. The space, the constraints, the condensation, the solvers and the point evaluation already exist. The new code is the vector assembly with the C : ε forms, the per-component Dirichlet data, the bordered rigid-mode system, and the outputs.
    • A second physics on the same meshes. Voxelspaetzle can drop its own P1 tetrahedral solver once S2 passes its references. The meshes come from Nurbforge or gmsh, as for the optical problems.
    • Memory and time are about 2–4× those of a real solver at the same size. Target problems (printed parts of 10–100 µm at p = 2–3) are around 10⁵–10⁶ unknowns, so MUMPS or cuDSS is needed at the upper end. This is the main cost of decision 3.
    • The optical coupling (S4) turns shrinkage into optical tolerances directly. The ADR-0011 velocity interface needs no change, because u lives on the same geometry nodes.
    • Locking for ν close to 0.5 is not addressed. The checks of §7 make that visible instead of failing silently.
    • Small strain only. Shrinkage of a few percent is within linear elasticity. Large rotations or strains would need a geometrically nonlinear formulation, which is not planned.

    Alternatives considered

    • A real-valued assembly and solver path from the start. It halves memory and cuts the time. But it duplicates the solver interface and the backends (dmumps, real cuDSS), breaks the uniform stack (kept factorisation, hpfem.opt), and contradicts CLAUDE.md §2.4 without a measured need. Deferred to §3 as an opt-in optimisation.
    • A dedicated vector H1 basis and DoF map (interleaved, node-major numbering). Interleaving helps band solvers, but the direct solvers reorder anyway. It would duplicate the hanging-node and p-refinement machinery that the component-major block reuses as it is.
    • Pinning DoFs to remove the rigid-body modes. It is simple, but it depends on the mesh and creates local stress concentrations. The Lagrange multipliers are exact and cost 6 extra rows.
    • A mixed u–p (displacement–pressure) formulation against locking. It is necessary only near ν = 0.5, it needs inf–sup stable pairs and a saddle-point solver, and it is not needed for the target polymers. It can be added later as a separate ADR.
    • An external FEM package (FEniCS, deal.II) called from Python. It brings a second mesh pipeline and a second set of conventions, and loses the coupling to the optical solvers on the same mesh, which is the point of #213 (§6, S4).
