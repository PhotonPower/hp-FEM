Detailed Description

Planar stacks of crystal layers (ADR-0015 §5, M19 S4): plane-wave reflection and transmission of layers and a substrate with symmetric permittivity tensors in the laboratory frame (x, y in the layer plane, z the layer normal; the wave comes from an isotropic, lossless incidence medium above the top interface and travels towards −z). Convention exp(−iωt): a lossy medium has a positive semi-definite Im ε.

Per region the in-plane wavevector fixes the Berreman system \( \psi' = ik_0K\psi \) for the tangential fields \( \psi = (E_x, E_y, \tilde H_x, \tilde H_y) \), \( \tilde H = Z_0H \), with \( E_z, H_z \) eliminated (this needs \( \varepsilon_{zz} \ne 0 \)). K is never diagonalised: two ordered complex Schur forms per region give orthonormal bases of the upward and the downward invariant subspaces and the triangular 2×2 blocks of K on them, which stay well defined for degenerate modes (isotropic layers, an optic axis along z) and at defective points (Voigt waves of absorbing biaxial media). Layers are crossed with the 2×2 exponentials of these blocks, and the reflection matrices are built by a stable S-matrix recursion from the substrate upwards that only ever multiplies decaying exponentials. Theory: docs/theory/anisotropic-stack.md.
